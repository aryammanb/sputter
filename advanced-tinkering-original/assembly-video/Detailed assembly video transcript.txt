0:00 The original magnetron
This is the reconstruction of Advanced Tinkering's original magnetron, not our separate redesign. The complete study assembly contains one hundred and twenty solid component instances. We will build it up in functional groups and inspect the connections. The supplied machined shapes retain their original geometry. Missing fasteners and service components are reconstructed or marked as reference parts. The animation explains the CAD assembly; it is not a qualified manufacturing or operating procedure.

0:30 Read the layered construction
Here are the main layers, separated along their common axis for inspection. Starting at the rear, we have the bottom body, the main PTFE ring, and the intermediate spacer. Above them sit the steel return yoke, magnets, copper closure, and copper cooling body. The target and its retaining ring are at the front, followed by the dark space shield. The long outer tube and small hardware are hidden in this view so the layer relationships are clear.

0:58 Inspect the two copper faces
The two copper parts do different jobs. The cooling body supports the target on its front face. Its rear face contains the blind magnet wells and the annular water channel. The backplate closes that channel and carries the two coolant connections. For inspection, the backplate is turned over beside the cooling body. This is only a viewing pose. The matching faces return to their original orientation when assembled.

1:25 Fit the two water seals
Two O rings sit in the front face of the copper backplate. The fourteen point five by two millimetre inner ring isolates the water from the center magnet region. The thirty five by two millimetre outer ring isolates it from the outer magnets. The faces close onto the one point four five millimetre gland depth, giving twenty seven point five percent nominal axial squeeze. The displayed ring shapes illustrate the installed occupied volume; they are not a prediction of elastomer deformation.

1:54 Secure the cooling enclosure
Twelve screws enter from the rear of the copper backplate and engage the cooling body. Their heads sit in the backplate counterbores, leaving room for the steel yoke. In this reconstruction they are represented as M three by sixteen socket head screws, selected to fit the measured stack. The actual author screw lengths were not supplied. These screws hold the two copper parts together; the two O rings provide the face seals. Changing a target does not require opening this joint.

2:23 Install the complete magnet pack
The original design uses twelve outer rod magnets on a fifty millimetre pitch circle. Each is eight millimetres in diameter and twenty millimetres long. Two twelve by ten millimetre discs form the center stack. The magnet pack enters the aligned dry bores in the copper parts. The center stack faces north toward the target and the outer rods face south, as shown in the source video. The tips stop half a millimetre short of the blind well ends. Magnetic forces are not simulated here.

2:53 Yoke, fittings and electrical contact
The mild steel yoke contacts the rear magnet faces and provides a magnetic return path. A separate retaining screw secures it to the copper backplate. The source model has a thread sized bore in both members at that screw, so the fastening detail remains flagged rather than silently redesigned. Two coolant fittings pass through the larger yoke clearances. The high voltage conductor is shown hooked under its separate copper terminal screw. Fitting identity and the exact wire shape are reference representations.

3:23 Add the spacer and outer seal
The intermediate spacer surrounds the copper closure and magnet yoke. Before bringing it against the cooling body, the seventy six by two millimetre ring occupies its front face groove. This is a different seal from the two water O rings. It closes the dry interior against the vacuum facing periphery. The spacer advances around the yoke until its front face meets the copper body. The shared source coordinates define that fitted position.

3:50 Attach the spacer to the copper
Twelve long screws pass through the spacer and engage the copper cooling body. Their heads are on the rear face of the spacer. The reconstructed M three by thirty five length provides engagement in the measured copper holes. These are not the twelve screws that later attach the bottom body. The two families use different bolt circles. Additional holes in the main PTFE ring provide clearance around these head attachment screw heads.

4:16 Seat the main PTFE ring
A sixty two by two millimetre O ring seals the spacer to the main PTFE insulator. The stepped collar locates in the spacer opening, and the large ring face seats against it. This part has two sets of holes: clearance for the head attachment screw heads, and passages for the separate base attachment screws. The PTFE separates the live upper assembly from the grounded bottom body. Its geometric fit alone does not establish an electrical operating rating.

4:44 Prepare the twelve screw sleeves
The bottom body also needs insulation around its twelve attachment screws. Each shoulder sleeve lines a through hole and places a PTFE flange beneath the screw head. The main insulator ring does not replace these individual sleeves. Their reconstructed shape fits the measured five point eight millimetre bores and eight millimetre counterbores. The exact author sleeve drawing was not supplied, so the flange thickness and profile are identified as fitted reconstruction dimensions.

5:12 Close the bottom-body joint
The second sixty two by two millimetre ring sits in the top face of the grounded bottom body. The body and its prepared sleeves advance onto the main PTFE ring. Twelve separate base screws pass through the sleeves and insulator, then engage the intermediate spacer. Their heads remain electrically separated from the grounded body by the sleeve flanges. The source video describes arcing concerns around this region; reproducing the CAD geometry does not resolve those concerns or establish safe clearances.

5:42 Assemble the KF16 connection
The central tube attaches beneath the bottom body using a KF sixteen style joint. The centering ring locates the seal, the tube flange approaches, and two semicircular clamp halves close around it. Six screws draw the clamp against the base. The source hole geometry supports M five sized fasteners. The flange and seal models are compatible reference shapes, and the displayed two hundred millimetre tube length is not an original measured dimension. The flexible services are threaded through this route before their connections are finalized.

6:14 Follow the annular cooling circuit
Now reveal the cooling space. Water comes through one hose and fitting into the copper annulus, then returns through the opposite port. Because the original passage is a continuous ring, water can travel around both semicircles. There is no C channel divider in this design. The inner and outer O rings separate the water from the dry magnet regions. Moving markers illustrate the possible routes; they do not represent a computed flow split, velocity, pressure loss, or cooling performance.

6:45 Separate water, air and vacuum
There are three different regions to keep straight. Water is confined to the annular cooling circuit and its connections. The dry interior communicates with room atmosphere through the central service tube. The hoses and high voltage cable run through that atmospheric space. Chamber vacuum is outside the sealed stack, including the space beneath the outer ground shield. The outer shield tube is not the primary pressure boundary. The face seals through the copper, spacer, PTFE ring, and bottom body establish that boundary.

7:16 Fit the target and retaining ring
At the front, the two inch target seats on the copper surface. The retaining ring overlaps its edge, and twelve front screws draw the ring down. The fitted titanium study target is two millimetres thick, matching the source clamp contact plane. That thickness is inferred, not a supplied target measurement. The ring keeps clearance from the outer copper face so it can apply target load. These front screws are independent of the rear cooling enclosure screws, allowing target changes without dismantling the water seals.

7:48 Complete the grounded shield
The outer ground shield tube slides over the assembled head. Three radial screws fasten it to the bottom body. The upper dark space shield then locates inside the tube and is retained by six smaller radial screws. Its opening exposes the target region while covering the surrounding hardware. The source tube has clearance and slots rather than a precision sliding seal. This shield belongs to the grounded structure, and its fit must not be confused with the face seals that keep the dry interior at atmosphere.

8:18 The complete reconstructed assembly
This completes the study assembly. It contains one hundred and twenty solid instances, including sixty five screws, twelve outer rods, two center discs, the five specified face O rings, and the reference KF seal. All solid bodies passed the native validity check. The documented interference regions are nominal screw shafts in thread sized bores, including the flagged yoke detail. The original machined geometry is preserved. Missing purchased hardware, sleeve dimensions, target thickness, and flexible routing remain labeled so this reconstruction can be studied without presenting estimates as original specifications.
