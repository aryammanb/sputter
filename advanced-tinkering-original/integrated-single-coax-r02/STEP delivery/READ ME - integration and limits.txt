INTEGRATED SINGLE COAX MAGNETRON R02
Mechanical integration study | 22 November 2026 | dimensions in millimetres

Open 'Integrated single coax magnetron R02.step' as an assembly in SolidWorks.
It contains 129 named component instances: 114 retained reconstruction parts
and 15 new or modified parts. Separate STEP files for those 15 parts and a
component BOM are included. Original source files have not been overwritten.
Native SLDASM conversion was not completed because the running SolidWorks
instances were unresponsive; this package is a STEP delivery.

CONNECTION LOGIC
One continuous coax enters the atmospheric service tube. A short exposed braid
band contacts a shield clamp on a conductive carrier anchored to the grounded
Boden. The protected center continues to an M4 ring terminal on the live copper
Backplate. A copper stand-off brings that connection behind the magnet yoke.
The original PTFE body isolation and the two reference coolant hose routes are
retained. No additional gun-end SMA connector or vacuum RF feedthrough is used.
The grounded body still needs a verified chamber/protective-ground bond; the
O-ring tube gland is not assumed to provide that bond.

WHAT CHANGED
Replaced the original Boden with the same source geometry plus one internal
blind ground-anchor pilot. Replaced the original illustrative HV hook/wire and
M4x8 terminal screw with the coax termination and M4x20 screw. Replaced the
reference 20 mm service tube with a 25.4 OD / 22.4 ID tube, retaining the source
KF16 flange capture and 20 OD / 16 ID neck at the head. The taper occurs beyond
the original clamp. Original yoke, magnets and water-seal grooves are unchanged.
The chamber compression adapter is not modeled; its actual bore, tube finish,
seal and insertion travel must be checked against the purchased adapter.

GEOMETRY CHECKS AND CONTACT INTERPRETATION
All 15 new/modified parts are valid single solids. New parts were checked for
volume interference against the 114 retained source solids and each other.
Four remaining modeled contacts are intentional representations:
1. M4 screw versus source copper thread-minor bore: 25.4679 cubic mm.
2. M3 anchor versus nominal tapped pilot: 7.37657 cubic mm.
3. Spring jaw versus braid: 1.28775 cubic mm, representing installed pressure.
4. Conformal saddle versus Boden: 0.04152 cubic mm, a formed-contact allowance.
No other new volume intersections were found at the recorded checking tolerance.
These allowances are not a thread, spring or forming simulation. Fabrication
drawings must resolve actual thread standards, tolerances and seated contact.
The two original coolant-hose reference solids clear the new parts.
STEP reimport and volume checks are recorded separately in STEP-verification.json.
Retained source reconstruction assumptions and nominal-thread contacts remain.

RELEASE LIMITS
This is a mechanical integration model, not a released RF gun. Frequency,
maximum RF voltage/current, DC self-bias and power remain unspecified. Cable,
lug, clamp and guard insulation are not electrically approved by this model.
Short exposed termination length, bonding impedance, heating and insulation
under the actual waveform need engineering checks and prototype verification.
Geometric metal separation does not establish a voltage or air-clearance rating.
Provide external cable/hose strain relief; the shield contact is not assumed
to be a complete cable-retention system. Actual crimp deformation is not modeled.
Original reference hose and service lengths, utility fittings and target
details retain the uncertainties of the initial video-based reconstruction.
Cooling flow, leak-tightness and thermal capability have not been validated.
