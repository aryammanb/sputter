SINGLE CONTINUOUS COAX - VISUAL CONNECTION PROPOSAL

Ground / RF return
Expose a short shield section near the grounded Boden, capture it with a conductive circumferential clamp, and bond that clamp to the body with a short, wide strap. The strap has a saddle-shaped foot following the native inner cone. A proposed blind anchor is shown on the dry-cavity side. This is new design geometry, not an existing author feature or a released hole position.

RF center
Continue the center conductor with dielectric protection beyond the shield termination and connect to a secure ring lug at the separate existing M4 live terminal on the copper Backplate. The stainless spacer is live because it is bolted to the copper assembly; do not use it as the shield landing. Only the small terminal connection should be exposed.

Isolation
The coax dielectric separates center from shield; the main PTFE ring and existing screw insulation separate the live spacer/cathode stack from the Boden. No isolation transformer or extra SMA interface is implied. RF voltage is between center and return; external and internal lengths of the same continuous coax are not separate isolated supplies.

Grounding boundary
This RF shield termination does not substitute for a verified protective/chassis bond between chamber ground, the Boden and outer ground shield. Do not assume the elastomer-sealed sliding gland supplies that electrical bond. The chamber and any separate bonding hardware are not shown in these cutaway views.

Geometry and limits
The original source CAD bodies are shown as decimated display geometry with a section cut. Magnets, coolant hoses and outer shield are hidden. New coax, clamp, strap, anchor and ring lug are explanatory overlays, not qualified SolidWorks parts. No original source CAD is overwritten. The cone location used for the ground saddle is source Y=-60mm, radius20mm at the centerline section; original source geometry gives radius = sourceY+80 across the chosen section. The proposed anchor's final position and blind depth must avoid original counterbores, screw holes and sealing surfaces and must not breach a vacuum boundary. Final RF voltage/current ratings, local dielectric thickness and air clearances, mechanical restraint and coolant routing need detailed design and verification before manufacture.
