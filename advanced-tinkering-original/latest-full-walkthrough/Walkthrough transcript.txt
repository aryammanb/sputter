0:00 The current complete magnetron
Here is the current magnetron as one complete C A D assembly. This walkthrough combines the original Advanced Tinkering head with our single coax power connection, one inch service tube and twelve one piece P T F E screw insulators with outward lips. The assembly contains one hundred and twenty nine component instances. We will separate its functional layers, inspect what each part does, and bring the gun back together. Proposed changes and reference purchased parts remain identified as study geometry.

0:30 Read the exploded stack
This exploded view shows the main layers along their shared axis. At the target end are the dark space shield, target clamp and two inch target. The copper cooling body and copper backplate form the cooled cathode. The magnets sit in dry wells and touch the steel return yoke. The spacer surrounds that pack. Farther back, the main P T F E ring separates the live spacer from grounded Boden. The long services and most small fasteners are temporarily hidden so the stack is readable.

1:01 The two copper parts
The two copper parts are now separated and the backplate is turned for inspection. This is a viewing pose. The cooling body provides the target contact face, the annular cooling channel and the blind magnet wells. The backplate closes the channel and carries the coolant ports. The magnet wells remain dry. The twelve rear screws join these copper parts independently of the target retaining screws. That separation allows target changes without opening the water enclosure. Actual target cooling still depends on the quality of its contact with copper.

1:34 Water seals and the cooling route
Two O rings separate the water channel from the dry magnet spaces. Their nominal sizes are fourteen point five by two millimetres and thirty five by two millimetres. The backplate closes onto the cooling body and the rear screws retain the joint. The revealed annulus has opposed inlet and outlet ports, so water can take both semicircular routes. Moving markers illustrate those routes; they are not a flow simulation. The displayed installed O ring shapes are occupied volume representations, not calculated rubber deformation.

2:07 Magnets and the return yoke
The magnet pack approaches from behind. Twelve outer rods sit on a fifty millimetre pitch circle. Two stacked discs form the center magnet. The center target facing pole is north and the outer target facing poles are south. The mild steel yoke supports the rear faces and provides the magnetic return path. A separate screw retains the yoke. The creator describes this as a Type two unbalanced arrangement. We retain it as the baseline; this C A D view does not calculate field strength, magnetic balance or a power rating.

2:39 Spacer, main insulator and vacuum seals
The stainless spacer surrounds the backplate and magnet yoke. A seventy six by two millimetre O ring seals its joint to the cooling body. Twelve long screws secure this live upper assembly. The main P T F E ring then sits between the spacer and Boden. A sixty two by two millimetre seal is used at each side of that insulating joint. These seals close the dry internal space against chamber vacuum. The main P T F E ring has both locating features and separate clearances for the different screw families.

3:11 The twelve captured PTFE cups
Here is the updated bottom body with twelve one piece P T F E insulators. Each insulator combines the shank sleeve, the shoulder beneath the metal screw head, the guard wall and the outward lip. The shoulder is captured by the screw, so the part does not depend on a separate guard fixing. The lip emerges from the hole and extends sideways over the grounded face. The proposed head pockets are enlarged to twelve millimetres. These base screws engage the live spacer, so their heads are also live. Their guards remain an unqualified electrical insulation proposal.

3:45 The single coax RF connection
One continuous coax runs through the atmospheric service tube. A short exposed shield band contacts the clamp on a conductive carrier anchored to Boden. The protected center continues separately to the live copper backplate. A copper stand off passes through the existing yoke clearance, bringing the larger ring terminal behind the yoke. A longer M four terminal screw retains it. The two blue coolant hoses remain beside this route. Clamp and terminal shapes use catalogue dimensions as reference envelopes. The cable and complete connection still need electrical selection and verification.

4:20 Service tube and mounting connection
Below Boden, a centering ring, seal, tube flange and two clamp halves form the K F sixteen style connection. Six screws retain the clamp. The revised tube preserves the smaller neck at this interface, then expands to a twenty five point four millimetre outside diameter for the selected one inch chamber compression adapter. The two coolant hoses and coax share its atmospheric interior. The purchased chamber adapter is not modeled here. Tube length, coolant fittings and the compatible K F seal remain reference details rather than confirmed supplier C A D.

4:54 Target, clamp and grounded shields
At the front, the two inch target seats on copper and the retaining ring overlaps its edge. Twelve separate front screws apply the clamping load. The displayed two millimetre target thickness is a reconstruction placeholder. Next, the grounded outer tube surrounds the head, and three radial screws secure it to Boden. The dark space shield locates at the target end and is retained by six smaller radial screws. Its opening exposes the target while screening surrounding hardware. Neither outer shield is the primary vacuum seal. These exploded motions are explanatory, not a qualified workshop procedure.

5:31 Water, atmosphere and chamber vacuum
The final cutaway brings the whole design together. Water occupies the copper annulus and connected hoses. The dry interior and central service tube communicate with room atmosphere. Chamber vacuum is outside the sealed stack, including beneath the grounded shield. The face seals define that pressure boundary. The copper head, spacer and target are electrically live; Boden and the shields are grounded. Our latest insulators and coax are integrated mechanically here. Magnetic performance, cooling capacity, RF ratings and arc resistance still require verification. The view then returns to the complete exterior.
