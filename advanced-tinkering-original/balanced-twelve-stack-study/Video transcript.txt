0:00 Same gun envelope, revised cooling head
This comparison uses the latest R zero three assembly as the baseline. The proposal keeps the same external gun envelope and target position. The changes are concentrated in the copper cooling body, copper backplate, steel return yoke, magnets, two water seals, and the upper coolant routing. This is a mechanical accommodation study. Balanced magnetic operation has not yet been simulated or demonstrated.

0:26 The proposed standard magnet pack
The original pack has twelve eight millimetre rods and two twelve millimetre centre discs. The proposal uses two six by ten millimetre discs at each outer position, and two twenty by ten millimetre discs at the centre. All are intended to be axially magnetized N forty two. The pack length remains twenty millimetres. The revised outer pitch circle is fifty eight millimetres. The pole area ratio is one point zero eight, which is only a screening estimate.

0:55 Why enlarging only the hole fails
Here the right hand view deliberately puts the larger centre magnet into the unmodified head. A twenty one millimetre clearance bore would leave only zero point five five millimetres to the old coolant channel. More decisively, it removes the original inner seal groove. The original coolant port counterbores also overlap the enlarged bore. So enlarging only the centre hole is not a workable conversion.

1:19 Cooling body: move the water annulus
The revised copper cooling body moves the water annulus outward while keeping its five millimetre width and seven millimetre depth. The centre blind well becomes twenty one millimetres in diameter. The outer wells become six point four millimetres and move outward four millimetres radially. The external profile, target seat, and existing attachment patterns are preserved. The larger channel volume does not prove cooling performance; that still needs thermal and flow analysis.

1:47 Backplate: two new seals and moved ports
The backplate needs matching magnet holes, new seal grooves, and moved coolant connections. The nominal inner water seal changes to twenty three by two, and the outer to forty three by two. The port axes move four point four five millimetres outward on each side. The existing five millimetre through passages and fitting counterbore diameters are retained. The two millimetre seal section and nominal gland depth are retained, but these remain seal design candidates rather than a qualified sealing system.

2:17 Yoke and coolant routes follow the ports
Moving the coolant ports also moves the fitting clearance holes in the steel yoke. Its outside diameter, thickness, and attachment positions can stay unchanged in this study. The reference fitting envelopes move with the ports, and the upper hose bends are rerouted. The lower service tube and RF connection stay in their existing positions. The actual purchased fittings, hose bend radius, yoke saturation, and electrical performance still need confirmation.

2:44 Local head redesign, not a new whole gun
The result is a local redesign of the magnetic and cooling head, rather than a redesign of the complete gun. The enlarged centre magnet can be accommodated inside the original external envelope, but it triggers coordinated changes to the water channel, both water seals, coolant ports, outer magnet locations, and yoke clearances. The target system, spacer, insulation, base, shields, mounting tube, and RF hardware are retained geometrically. The next engineering stage is magnetic and thermal validation before fabrication.
