# Cooling body and cover — assembly walkthrough

Actual F01-001 and F01-002 Rev A CAD meshes. Colors distinguish the two copper parts. Seal traces, screw markers, alignment guides and flow markers are explanatory overlays. No seal deformation or fluid simulation is claimed.

## 0:00 — Two parts, one cooling enclosure

Here are our two actual CAD parts. Orange is the cooling body; teal is the cover. Both are copper. The cover approaches the channel side of the body. We will inspect the mating features, show the seal locations, align the parts, and bring them together.

## 0:18 — Inspect the mating faces

First, turn the cover over so both mating faces are visible. The body carries the water channel and two circular O ring grooves. The cover has two water holes and a short pocket for the proposed divider strip. This flipped position is only for inspection; the cover must rotate back before assembly.

## 0:37 — Position the seals

The green traces mark the inner and outer O ring positions. They separate the wet annular region from its surroundings. The gold trace marks the proposed internal divider strip. Its job is to reduce the inlet to outlet shortcut. These are seal location graphics, not finished seal models or a compression simulation.

## 0:57 — Align the cover

Now rotate the cover into its installation orientation, with the fitting bosses pointing rearward. The guide lines connect each cover water hole to the corresponding channel end. The six clearance holes must align with the body's six receiving holes. The divider pocket must also face the solid channel bridge. Matching the circular bolt pattern alone is not enough.

## 1:20 — Close the mating faces

Slide the cover straight toward the body without twisting the seals out of position. At zero gap, the flat metal lands meet at their shared mating plane. The circular grooves remain one point five millimetres deep for the proposed two millimetre rings, giving twenty five percent nominal squeeze. Actual seal compression and cover stiffness still require verification.

## 1:42 — Connect with six screws

Six screws enter from the rear, pass through the cover clearance holes, and engage the intended M three threads in the body. The highlighted circles show their locations; actual screws are not included. Tightening should be developed in opposing steps with a validated load and torque. The cover screws are separate from the front target clamp screws.

## 2:03 — Follow the internal water route

With the cover hidden for this internal view, follow the intended water route. Water passes through one boss and cover hole, travels around the C shaped channel, then returns through the other hole and boss. The bridge and proposed strip discourage a shortcut. The moving dots are explanatory, not a fluid simulation. Bypass and pressure loss must be measured.

## 2:26 — The assembled pair

This is the fitted pair. The flat front face supports the future target, while the cover remains attached during target changes. The plates together are ten millimetres thick, with the bosses projecting another eight millimetres rearward. This is a nominal CAD fit, not a pressure qualified assembly. Fittings, seals, fasteners, and the surrounding vacuum and electrical insulation still need completion.
