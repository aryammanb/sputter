# Copper cooling body — narrated walkthrough

Actual F01-001 Rev A CAD geometry. English synthetic narration. Flow markers and target outlines are explanatory overlays, not CFD or additional manufactured parts.

## 0:00 — The copper cooling body

This is the actual three dimensional model of our first component: the copper cooling body, revision A. It is seventy millimetres in diameter and seven millimetres thick. We will look at the target face, water channel, seals, and fasteners, and explain why each feature is there. The geometry is modeled; its operating performance is not yet validated.

## 0:26 — Where the target sits

This flat front face supports the two inch target. The circular outline marks its fifty point eight millimetre footprint; it is an explanatory overlay, not another modeled component. Heat must cross the contact between target and copper, so flatness and even clamping matter. We have left target thickness open. The target itself does not seal the cooling water.

## 0:53 — Why the body is wider

The copper body extends nine point six millimetres radially beyond the nominal target edge. That gives us space to develop the clamp, fasteners, and surrounding interfaces. Seventy millimetres is a packaging choice, not an optimized or minimum diameter. Keeping the target seat flat avoids locking this first revision to a close fitting target pocket. The future clamp must still locate the target.

## 1:21 — The rear water channel

Now turn to the rear. The blue region is the actual machined water channel. It spans radii of eleven to twenty five millimetres and is three millimetres deep, with rounded seven millimetre end caps. Water is intended to enter at one end, sweep around behind the target, and leave at the other. The moving markers show intended direction only, not a flow simulation. The ports belong in the next component: the cover.

## 1:52 — The bridge controls the route

The solid bridge between the channel ends separates the intended inlet and outlet positions. Without it, water could take a short route instead of sweeping around the target. But metal contact against the future cover does not automatically seal that bridge. We must design the cover and divider interface to control bypass. The two circular O rings provide containment, not a divider seal.

## 2:19 — The copper wall beneath the target

This cutaway exposes the four millimetre copper wall between the channel floor and target seat. It keeps water separate from the vacuum beneath the target and conducts heat into the coolant. A thinner wall brings the magnets closer, but increases concerns about pressure deflection and face distortion. A thicker wall increases magnet spacing. Four millimetres is our first iteration, pending structural, thermal, and magnetic checks.

## 2:49 — Two O-ring grooves

The two highlighted circular grooves accept the inner and outer face seals against the future cover. Candidate rings have fourteen and fifty four millimetre inside diameters, both with two millimetre cross sections. Each groove is two point six millimetres wide and one point five deep. A cover seated on the lands gives twenty five percent nominal squeeze and about eighty one percent gland fill. These are nominal geometry values. Seal material, tolerances, swelling, temperature, and cover deflection still need checking.

## 3:26 — Six holes retain the coolant cover

These six rear holes are for fastening the coolant cover. They are equally spaced on a sixty four millimetre bolt circle, outside the outer O ring. The model shows two point five millimetre pilot pockets, four millimetres deep, for intended M three threads. Finished threads and drill points are not modeled. Copper thread strength, useful engagement, and tightening load must be checked before manufacture. These screws retain the cover; the O rings make the seal.

## 3:59 — Three holes retain the target clamp

On the front are three separate pilot pockets for the target clamp. They sit on the same bolt circle but are rotated between the rear holes, so the two patterns do not meet. Their modeled depth is three millimetres. Separating the clamp fasteners from the coolant cover lets us change the target without opening the water circuit. The clamp itself, thread details, and target locating features are still to be designed.

## 4:28 — What this revision establishes

This revision establishes the target seat, cooling passage, seal grooves, and separate fastening interfaces. The native part is one solid, and its volume matches an independent calculation. It does not yet form a closed water circuit or an atmospheric magnet cavity. The next part is the coolant cover, including the ports and divider treatment. We still need pressure, leak, thermal, magnetic, and electrical verification before this becomes a build ready gun.
