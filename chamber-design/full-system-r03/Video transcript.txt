0:00 Complete system and selected pumping layout
This revision brings the chamber, gun, pumps, gauges and gas connection into one assembly. The chamber remains two hundred by two hundred by three hundred millimetres internally. We have retained the simpler pumping arrangement: chamber to turbo, then through a flexible foreline to one mechanical backing pump. There is no separate roughing bypass or high vacuum isolation valve in this revision. The gas supply model ends at the six millimetre push fitting; the regulator and cylinder remain outside this design scope.

0:31 Front loading and fixed rear services
The front door opens without moving the gun or any rear service connection. The clear opening is one hundred and eighty by two hundred and seventy millimetres. The viewport opening is seventy diameter. Both pressure gauges and the mass flow controller connect at the rear. The left wall remains blank, while the two spare right side K F twenty five ports now have removable blanking caps. Next, the integrated close up shows how the gun compression fitting attaches and how its cap sets the gun height.

1:01 Two different connections
There are two different connections here. The K F clamp joins the compression fitting to the welded chamber flange. The threaded cap at the top has a different job: it compresses an O ring around the gun tube. I have replaced the single solid placeholder with a separate body, threaded cap, follower and tube seal. The detailed thread and seal seat are illustrative, pending the actual purchased fitting drawing.

1:25 Seat the fitting on the KF flange
First, the fitting body sits on the chamber K F flange, with the face seal between them. This face seal is separate from the O ring around the tube. The body is lowered into place, while the compression cap remains loose. We are showing the mechanical assembly with the chamber vented.

1:43 Close the KF clamp
The two clamp jaws close around the mating flange rims. Tightening the wing nut secures this joint. From now on, the body stays fixed to the chamber. You do not loosen this K F clamp to adjust the gun height. Height adjustment happens at the separate threaded compression cap.

2:00 Feed the tube through the loose fitting
With the upper cap loose, feed the gun service tube upward from inside the chamber. The gun head stays inside. The twenty five point four millimetre tube slides through the fitting. This animation follows the existing gun geometry; the roof and body remain stationary.

2:18 Choose the target to substrate distance
Now move the complete gun up or down to choose the target to substrate distance. In our chamber model, the working range is seventy five to one hundred and fifty millimetres. Here we return to one hundred. The body and K F clamp stay still. Support the gun while the cap is loose.

2:36 Thread the cap down to grip the tube
Hold the tube at the selected position and turn the cap clockwise, viewed from above. The cap advances down the body thread. Its shoulder pushes the follower onto the O ring, squeezing the seal against the tube. The tube does not rotate with the cap. The model uses an illustrative two millimetre thread pitch, so two turns produce four millimetres of cap travel. That is an animation dimension, not a tightening instruction for the purchased part.

3:04 Section view of the compression mechanism
The section view shows the load path. The moving cap shoulder presses the follower, which compresses the tube O ring. The lower face seal and K F clamp remain undisturbed. O ring deformation is shown schematically to explain the mechanism; it is not a seal stress calculation. The actual mating flange and centering ring must also have enough clear bore for the tube.

3:26 Stage, shutter and exact working dimensions
Inside the chamber, the ninety diameter substrate stage is six millimetres thick, with its top at height forty. The one millimetre reference substrate puts the actual working surface at forty one. The target to substrate distance is adjustable from seventy five to one hundred and fifty, with one hundred as the default. The bent shutter is one millimetre sheet. Its flat midplanes are separated by two hundred and forty millimetres. The lower blade underside is at fifty three, exactly twelve above the substrate. It rotates eighty degrees to uncover the sample. These are the same nominal dimensions and checked motion envelope established in the chamber revision.

4:06 EXT70 model and ISO63 claw-clamp connection
The turbo exterior is reconstructed from the Edwards E X T seventy dimension drawing, rather than from an invented pump shape. Its I S O sixty three flange is ninety five diameter, and the reference overall height is one hundred and fifty seven point zero five. Four double claw clamps join the pump flange to the chamber flange, with a face seal between them. The chamber is carried by its own stand. The standard E X T seventy manual specifies a K F sixteen backing outlet, so this model includes a K F sixteen to K F twenty five adapter for the requested hose. If your actual pump has a K F twenty five outlet variant, that adapter is omitted.

4:46 KF25 hose and mechanical backing pump
The mechanical pump is an Edwards R V three reference, imported from the supplier's actual STEP file. It is a layout reference, not a confirmed purchase. The flexible K F twenty five hose connects the turbo backing outlet to the rotary pump suction inlet. This distinction matters: the other rotary pump port is its exhaust and is not connected back to the chamber. The hose routing is modeled as a curved core with corrugations and separate end flanges. The selected commercial hose must accommodate that route and its minimum bend radius. The blue moving markers illustrate the gas path, not a flow simulation.

5:21 Pirani and Penning gauge installation
The two lower rear ports carry the Pirani and Penning gauges. Their chamber port centres are at height ninety, fifty millimetres to either side of centre. Both use K F twenty five fittings. Each gauge is mounted behind a ninety degree elbow so its sensing opening does not look directly at the sputtering target. The housings and connectors are reference geometry because the actual gauge model numbers were not supplied. Their final mounting orientation, temperature and magnetic field clearances must follow the selected gauge manuals.

5:53 Mass flow controller and chamber connection
The upper rear port is the mass flow controller connection, at height two hundred and ten. A K F twenty five to female quarter inch V C R adapter connects to the controller's male V C R outlet, with a metal face seal gasket. Both controller ports are assumed male V C R, as requested. The reference body follows the H O R I B A E four forty J layout, including a one hundred and twenty four millimetre face to face distance. A rear shelf supports the controller independently so its weight is not carried by the gas fitting. The signal connector is shown on top; its electrical cable is outside the present model.

6:30 MFC inlet adapters through to the push fitting
On the supply side, the male V C R inlet meets a female V C R adapter assembly and its metal gasket. The other end of that adapter is quarter inch female N P T. Into it screws the quarter inch male N P T push fitting for six millimetre outside diameter tubing. This is the same adapter chain recovered from the earlier chat. V C R seals at the gasket face; N P T is a separate tapered thread interface. The exploded view separates those parts so their functions are visible. Gas would enter at the push fitting, pass through the controller, and then enter the chamber. The model stops at the push socket, exactly as requested.
